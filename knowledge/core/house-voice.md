# House voice

## The stance

A Braingames puzzle is made *for someone*. That is the whole premise, and it should be
felt rather than announced. The voice is:

- **Warm, not chummy.** We are pleased to see the solver. We do not high-five them.
- **Clever, not clever-clever.** A good pun earns a smile. A pun that needs explaining
  earns nothing.
- **Confident about difficulty.** Hard clues are not apologised for. Tier 3 is supposed
  to be hard; softening it insults the solver who chose it.
- **Culturally broad.** Reach across eras, geographies, and registers. A puzzle whose
  every proper noun is a 1970s American television actor is not general knowledge, it is
  one specific person's general knowledge.

## What we avoid

- **Crosswordese as a crutch.** `ESNE`, `OLIO`, `ETUI` and their friends exist to rescue
  a bad fill. Every one of them is a small tax on the solver's goodwill.
- **Themes about tragedy.** A theme is a celebration. Adjacent vocabulary in the fill is
  fine; a theme built on disaster, illness, or conflict is not.
- **Punching down.** No theme or clue whose humour depends on a group being the joke.
- **Recency bias.** A theme resting entirely on something from the last six months will
  read as dated within a year, and the archive is a permanent artifact.

## The post-solve note

Every puzzle ships with a short paragraph explaining the theme — what the trick was, why
these entries. This does more work than its length suggests: it is what makes the puzzle
feel *authored* rather than emitted, which is the difference between a personalized
product and an automated one.

Write it as a constructor talking to a solver who has just finished:

> Each theme answer is a common expression that also works as a trail direction — which
> is how `TAKE THE HIGH ROAD` ended up next to `GO OFF THE BEATEN PATH`. The revealer,
> `SCENIC ROUTE`, is the long way round, which felt right for a puzzle that takes its
> time getting to the point.

Not:

> This puzzle features a theme based on hiking-related idioms. There are four theme
> answers plus a revealer.

The first tells the solver something they did not already know. The second describes the
puzzle they just solved back to them.

## Register by cohort

The cohort brief specifies a clue voice. Follow it, but stay recognisably Braingames — a
cohort brief adjusts the dial, it does not replace the stance above. A cohort that loves
groan-puns gets more groan-puns; it does not get a different set of ethics about what a
theme can be about.
